**A useful website design quotation tells you what will be built, what you will pay now and what you will pay again later.** Send suppliers the same written brief; otherwise, two quotes for “five pages” may describe different work.

## The brief to send before asking for quotes

Describe what a customer should be able to do on the finished site. “A modern website” leaves each supplier to interpret the work. Include:

1. **Business and customer:** what you sell, where you serve customers and who will use the site.
2. **Main action:** call, start a WhatsApp conversation, submit a request, book a confirmed appointment or buy online.
3. **Pages:** list them by name. Five service sections on one page are different from five separate service pages.
4. **Features:** specify forms, an editable blog, catalogue, payments, logins, languages or integrations.
5. **Content:** identify the text, photos and logo you already have, and the material you need help producing.
6. **Existing setup:** list any domain, hosting, website or business email that must keep working during the change.
7. **Timing and handover:** state your desired launch date and ask what depends on your approvals, access or content.

## How to compare website design quotes

Put supplier answers side by side using our [free quote-comparison worksheet](/downloads/website-quote-comparison.csv). Its supplier columns are blank: an unknown charge is not free.

| Compare | Ask each supplier | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| One-time work | Which pages and features does this price deliver? | A brochure site and an editable catalogue are different scopes |
| Content | Who writes, uploads and approves text and images? | Missing content can delay launch or require extra work |
| Enquiry flow | Does the form send, store or only prepare a message? | A success message alone does not establish delivery |
| Recurring costs | What renews, when and at what rate? | Introductory prices may differ from later years |
| Ownership | Who controls the domain, hosting, files and editing accounts? | Handover should explain your access and ability to move |
| Support | What is included, for how long and what costs extra? | Launch fixes and ongoing changes may have different terms |

For example, suppose one quote includes copywriting and another expects you to supply finished text. Ask the second supplier to price that work, or decide that you will write it. Repeat this check for each difference that matters before comparing totals.

Calculate **first-year and renewal costs separately**. Add the build once. Record domain, hosting, paid tools and selected care for the relevant period. Ask for tax treatment and the final payable total. If the renewal rate is unconfirmed, the later-year total is still unknown.

## Questions to settle before accepting a quotation

A low quote is not automatically poor value, and a higher price does not prove quality. Resolve unclear promises in writing:

- **“SEO included”:** ask for launch deliverables. Titles, headings and crawl setup differ from an ongoing campaign; neither guarantees rankings.
- **“Unlimited changes”:** ask what counts as a revision, when approvals happen and how a new feature is priced.
- **“Free hosting”:** ask how long it is included, what renews, who controls the account and how the site can move later.
- **“You own the website”:** ask what is handed over, whether third-party licences transfer and which accounts you can access.
- **“Contact form included”:** ask for a demonstration of successful delivery to the agreed destination. A WhatsApp button opens a conversation; the visitor still needs to send the message.
- **“Ready in a week”:** identify which content, credentials and approvals must arrive first, and what happens if they are delayed.

Inspect delivered work on your own phone. Can you understand the offer, find credible examples and contact the business?
