Search for a website in Mahabubnagar and the first prices you will see start around ₹3,800. Ask a Hyderabad agency for the same thing and you may be quoted ten times that. Both are real offers. They are not the same product, and the difference is worth understanding before you spend anything.

## The specific thing that makes this district different

Your customers are on mid-range Android phones, often on a connection that drops between 4G and 3G without warning. That is not a detail — it decides what can be built.

A heavy template loaded with sliders, animation libraries and six web fonts will score well on a designer's laptop and take eleven seconds to open on a customer's phone outside a shop on a busy afternoon. They will not wait. That customer is gone and you will never know they existed.

We build light for this reason, not as a technical preference. It is the single most common failure we see in sites built for local businesses by people who have never watched a customer here try to use one.

## Why the ₹3,800 offers exist

They are not a scam. They are a genuinely different business model, and it is worth being clear about how it works so you can decide whether it suits you.

A template site is profitable at ₹3,800 because the design was built once and sold hundreds of times, the content is placeholder text you replace yourself, and the headline figure usually covers year one only. Nobody is losing money. You are buying a real thing at a fair price for what it is.

What you are not buying is anyone thinking about your business. Nobody asks which of your services actually make money, why customers choose you over the shop two doors down, or what people ring up to ask before they visit. Those answers are what turns a website into an enquiry, and they cannot be templated.

> The honest test: if a customer is choosing between you and two competitors, all three on their phone, does your site make you look like the most serious of the three? If yes, the cheap site did its job. If not, you paid ₹3,800 to look like the least serious.

## The costs nobody puts in the local quote

- **GST** at 18%, on top. Ours included.
- **Domain**, yours annually and forever — a few hundred to around fifteen hundred rupees a year at ordinary registrar rates.
- **Hosting**, also annual. Check the renewal rate, not the first-year offer.
- **Content** — photographs of your actual premises, your actual services, your actual prices. This is the one that stalls most projects.

## Local versus Hyderabad

A Hyderabad agency will usually quote higher, and the honest reason is overhead rather than talent — office rent, account managers, and a client list where you would be one of the smaller accounts.

The practical difference matters more. They will not walk into your shop. They will not know that the road outside is dug up for three months, or which weeks are dead here and which are frantic, or that half your customers find you through a WhatsApp group rather than through Google.

That is not an argument that local is always better. It is an argument that for a business whose customers are all within a few kilometres, someone who can visit is worth something real. ## Before you pay anyone

![Two people at a desk reviewing a printed quote, one ticking items off with a pen](/images/blog/checking-a-website-quote.webp "Five questions, asked before money changes hands, will tell you more than the figure at the bottom of the quote.")

1. Ask to see two sites they have already built, and **open both on your phone**. Count the seconds.
2. Ask who writes the words. If it is you, add several weeks to the timeline.
3. Ask what year two costs — domain, hosting, renewals, in writing.
4. Ask whether you own the site, the domain and the hosting account.
5. Ask what happens when something breaks, and what that costs.
