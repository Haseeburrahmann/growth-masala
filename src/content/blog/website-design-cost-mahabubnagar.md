## The specific thing that makes this district different

Many customers will open your site on a phone, sometimes on a slow connection. That should influence the design before any animation or large image is added.

A heavy template loaded with sliders, animation libraries and several web fonts may look fine on a designer's laptop but feel slow on a customer's phone. Ask to open a supplier's previous work on your own phone and connection before choosing them.

We build light for this reason. A quick test of a real delivered site tells you more than a speed promise in a quote.

## How to assess a low-price offer

A headline price alone cannot tell you the quality or scope of a website. Ask the supplier whether the price covers a template or a custom layout, who writes the content, what the page limit is and what happens after the first year. Compare the written answers instead of assuming a cheap quote excludes those items.

A small, clearly scoped site can be enough when customers only need to check your services and contact you. More complex navigation, integrations or content work may justify a different budget. The useful question is whether the quoted build meets your actual need.

## The costs nobody puts in the local quote

- **GST** at 18%, if applicable. Our published package prices exclude GST; ask for the final amount including tax.
- **Domain**, yours annually and forever — a few hundred to around fifteen hundred rupees a year at ordinary registrar rates.
- **Hosting**, also annual. Check the renewal rate, not the first-year offer.
- **Content** — photographs of your actual premises, your actual services, your actual prices. This is the one that stalls most projects.

## How to compare two website design quotes

Put both quotes into the same three-year calculation: **build price + GST + three years of domain and hosting + any required support or edit fees**. Ask each supplier to fill in the missing lines. A lower first-year price can become the higher total if hosting renewals or routine edits are expensive.

For each quote, also write down who supplies the text and photos, how many pages and revisions are included, whether the domain and hosting accounts are in your name, and what happens after the launch support period. These details explain more of the price difference than the city in the supplier's address.

## Before you pay anyone

![Two people at a desk reviewing a printed quote, one ticking items off with a pen](/images/blog/checking-a-website-quote.webp "Five questions, asked before money changes hands, will tell you more than the figure at the bottom of the quote.")

1. Ask to see two sites they have already built, and **open both on your phone**. Count the seconds.
2. Ask who writes the words. If it is you, add several weeks to the timeline.
3. Ask what year two costs — domain, hosting, renewals, in writing.
4. Ask whether you own the site, the domain and the hosting account.
5. Ask what happens when something breaks, and what that costs.
