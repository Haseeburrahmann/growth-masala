[SQC Solar & Loans](https://www.sqcsolarloans.com/) is a Mahabubnagar business with two distinct service areas. Growth Masala built its website around that distinction: visitors can choose solar power or loan services from the homepage, explore dedicated service pages, and contact the business by phone or WhatsApp.

This case study describes the delivered website and our analysis of its navigation. It does not report a client testimonial or a measured increase in enquiries.

## The user need: find the right service first

Someone exploring solar power arrives with different questions from someone looking for loan information. A single undifferentiated list makes both visitors work out which details apply to them.

The practical design brief was to give each audience a clear route while keeping SQC's services together on one website. The homepage presents the two paths before asking the visitor to read through individual offerings. That choice is visible in the delivered site; the reasoning here is our explanation of the design, rather than a quotation from the client.

For a business with different service lines, this is a useful question to ask before counting pages: what must a visitor choose before the rest of the information makes sense?

## How the navigation works

The split homepage acts as a starting point. A visitor selects the relevant service area and continues into dedicated pages instead of having to interpret every offering at once.

Those pages give the website a structure that can be linked directly. Someone sharing information about a particular service can send its page, rather than sending the homepage with instructions about where to look next.

Phone and WhatsApp contact options provide the next step when a visitor needs to discuss their situation. They are enquiry routes. Opening WhatsApp does not establish that a message was sent, and neither contact action demonstrates that an enquiry became a customer.

## What the website delivers

The visible build brings together three elements:

- A homepage that distinguishes solar power from loan services.
- Dedicated service pages that continue those separate journeys.
- Direct phone and WhatsApp contact options for further questions.

Our role in this case study is website development. The website's loan and solar information belongs to the client's business; this article does not assess loan eligibility, approve applications, or make claims about solar savings.

## What we have not measured

We are not publishing before-and-after traffic, search positions, enquiry totals or sales results for this project. Without a defined baseline and verified tracking, those would be unsupported claims. The evidence presented here is the delivered website and its observable structure.

Any later performance assessment should separate visits, contact clicks, enquiries received and customer outcomes. Those are different stages, and a rise in one does not prove a rise in the next. A website launch also does not guarantee Google rankings.

## A takeaway for buyers

When comparing website proposals, ask the supplier to show the route for each major audience: where they start, what they read, and how they make contact. Then check which pages and contact features are actually included in the quote.
